What is “risk per trade”?

Risk per trade is the amount of money you would lose if your stop loss is hit. Many traders express it as a percentage of their account balance and decide it before entering, so a single trade cannot do unplanned damage. For example, with a balance of 1,000 and a rule of 1% risk, the planned risk is 10. The percentage is your own decision; there is no universal right number.

How is position size calculated?

Position size links your risk amount to your stop distance: Lot size = Risk amount ÷ (Stop distance × Contract size). Example for Gold with a contract size of 100: risk 50, stop distance 5 → 50 ÷ (5 × 100) = 0.10 lot. Contract sizes and minimum lots differ between brokers, so check your broker’s specification.

What is an R-multiple?

R expresses a trade result in units of the risk you took. R-multiple = Profit/Loss ÷ Risk amount. If you risked 50 and made 100, that is +2R. If you lost the full 50, that is −1R. Looking at results in R makes trades of different sizes comparable.

What should I record in a trade journal?

At minimum: date, symbol, direction, entry, stop loss, exit, lot size and result. For discipline, also note whether you followed your plan, how you felt (calm, rushed, fearful) and one lesson. Review the journal weekly and look for repeating patterns, not single trades.
